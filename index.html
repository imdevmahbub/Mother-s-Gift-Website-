The Editorial Protocol

First Week Check

The first seven days after birth are a hormonal and physical sea change. There are no milestone charts here, no tracking ounces or tracking guilt.

This is a gentle rhythm check: practical, reassuring notes on pelvic rest, warmth, nutrition, and when to ask the companion to take over.

Curated with Certified Midwives
01

Horizontal Dignity

Stay in the bed. Walking around the house to “tidy up” delays pelvic floor vascular healing. Let dust sit. Let guests wait outside.

02

The Warmth Mandate

No iced drinks or cold raw salads. The body lost significant heat during labor. Sip hot ginger teas, consume simmering marrow broths.

03

Companion Action Point

Your concierge can enforce a gentle "Do Not Disturb" protocol for your doorstep so visitors don't wake the household.

01

Milk Arrives, Tears Flow

Estrogen and progesterone plunge rapidly. Crying without a discernible cause is physiologically normal. You are not broken.

02

Engorgement Relief

Gentle lymph massage toward the armpits, never hard kneading. Warm compresses before feeding, cold compresses after.

03

Companion Action Point

Text the concierge: “Lactation check.” We match an in-person or encrypted video consult within hours.

01

The Visitors Buffer

If someone visits to hold the baby while you do chores, decline. True visitors should wash a dish, bring a stew, and leave quietly.

02

First Fresh Air

Five minutes sitting on a porch chair under daylight is enough. Do not march around the neighborhood just yet.

03

Companion Action Point

Let us arrange your linen pickup so you don't face a mountain of laundry over the weekend.

Human Consent Architecture

Data, strictly on her terms.

New parents are ruthlessly monetized by algorithms and baby tracking tech. We take a radically quiet stand: zero surveillance, zero third-party brokers, zero marketing blasts.

01 / You Choose

Consent First

She dictates how we communicate: WhatsApp, simple SMS, email, or solely through a designated partner. If she asks for silence, we go silent.

02 / We Understand

Context, Not Data

We remember her dietary aversions, birth recovery nuances, and preferred nap hours so she never repeats herself. It is never sold or modeled.

03 / Care Adapts

Dynamic Cadence

If recovery is easy, care spaces out. If mastitis or sleep deprivation hits, our support intensifies gently to absorb the blow.

04 / Absolute Control

Permanent Wipe

When her postpartum companion window concludes, her communication history and private preferences can be purged with a single request.

The Mother’s Rule of Fives

5 Days in Bed Resting horizontally. Baby skin-to-skin. Only standing to move to bathroom.
5 Days Around the Bed Sitting in comfortable armchair, gentle nursing, warm bone broth by bedside.
5 Days Near the Bed Moving to living room sofa. Still not lifting heavy laundry or vacuuming.
